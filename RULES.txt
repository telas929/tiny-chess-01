Build the strongest chess engine in 4 KB of Python

The transcript checks have changed since these rules were written, on 11 November 2026 and after. Each entry is held to the checks in force when it is checked; where the Transcript gate section here differs from them, those checks apply. The checks as they stand today are in this file under "## The transcript gate", and at https://make.fun/api/competitions/tiny-chess-01/requirements.

## The task
Write a chess engine in at most 4,096 bytes of Python. Each trial it plays 8 games against the house gauntlet through the env at MAKEFUN_ENV_URL, one game after another, and the games' results are its score. It may read /input/prompt.txt (the path is in MAKEFUN_PROMPT_FILE), the brief: the trial's 8 games with their opponents, colours and openings, the env's routes and these rules in short. It exits when every game is over. Its environment also holds MAKEFUN_TRIAL_SEED, a seed for any randomness you need.

## The size rule
main.py's bytes as committed count, everything included: comments, whitespace, the coding line, and a compressed payload with the code that unpacks it. It must be at most 4,096 bytes. main.py alone is the engine: it may use Python's standard library, and it reads, imports, opens or runs no other file of your repository, and no data stored anywhere but in main.py's own bytes.

The rest of the repository is held small too: the whole snapshot (every file at the submitted commit, main.py, the license, a README and the claim file included; nothing under .git) is at most 8,192 bytes, and no file in it is over 4,096. A submission over either cap is refused at intake, with its sizes. A license must fit: MIT, BSD, ISC and 0BSD all do. A GPL-licensed or other long-licensed entry (a fork of sunfish, which is GPL-3.0-only) can come in only as a `makefun bundle` source upload whose LICENSE holds just its SPDX tag (SPDX-License-Identifier: GPL-3.0-only).

## The gauntlet
Eight house engines, each at a fixed strength, all the make.fun team's own code (MIT licensed; the env bundle in the kit holds them):
- depth-2, depth-3 and depth-4: an alpha-beta search to that many plies
- nodes-100k, nodes-250k, nodes-500k, nodes-1M and nodes-2M: the same search, deepened one ply at a time; a depth after the first that runs past 100,000, 250,000, 500,000, 1,000,000 or 2,000,000 nodes is dropped whole, and the last whole depth's move stands

The searches extend each leaf with a search over captures and promotions, and over every reply to a check; they order their moves by a transposition table, captures and killer moves, and evaluate material (pawn 100, knight 320, bishop 330, rook 500, queen 900) plus piece-square terms and the bishop pair; a repetition or the fifty-move rule inside the search scores 0. None reads a clock: the same position and history give the same move.

Their anchors, the ratings your Elo is measured against, come from a calibration round-robin on a ladder of eleven engines, these eight above a random mover, a greedy capturer and a depth-1 search: every pair at most 4 rungs apart (34 pairs) played each of the book's 40 openings with both colours under these rules, 2,720 games, and the ratings are the Bradley-Terry maximum-likelihood fit (a draw half a win and half a loss) with 2 virtual draws a pair, the random mover at 0. The games and the fit are in the kit with the generator (program/generator/calibration.json). The anchors: depth-2 855, depth-3 1073, depth-4 1199, nodes-100k 1392, nodes-250k 1497, nodes-500k 1596, nodes-1M 1667, nodes-2M 1712 (and below the trial's eight, random 0, greedy 348, depth-1 696).

## The games
A trial holds 8 games: one against each opponent, weakest first. Each game starts from its own opening of the book (40 well-known openings, each 8 plies), drawn without replacement for the trial, so the trial's 8 openings all differ. Your colour alternates from one opponent to the next, 4 games with white and 4 with black: on the 1st, 3rd and 5th scored instances and on the 1st and 3rd practice instances you have white against depth-2, black against depth-3 and so on up the ladder, and on the others the other way round, so two instances meet every opponent with both colours. A practice trial holds the first 6 of those games, against depth-2 to nodes-500k, 3 with white and 3 with black (see Trials). A game starts from the standard start position with its opening's 8 plies played for both sides; white moves next, so when you play black the house has made its move by the time you are served the position.

A game ends:
- by the rules: checkmate; a draw by stalemate, by insufficient material (no pawn, rook or queen left, and at most one knight or bishop, or only bishops all on squares of one colour), by the third occurrence of a position (the same pieces, side to move, castling rights and en passant capture), or by 100 plies with no capture and no pawn move. Each draw applies at once, with no claim; a move that mates wins even when it also makes the third occurrence or the hundredth ply.
- at the ply cap: a game still going after 200 plies from the start position (the opening's 8 included) is adjudicated, won by the side ahead in material by at least 5, counting pawn 1, knight 3, bishop 3, rook 5 and queen 9, and otherwise drawn.
- on time: your move must reach the env within 10,000 ms of the env serving the position (its answer to your last move, or to /game for a game's first position). A /move, /resign or /game for the game in play that comes later loses it on time. The house's own thinking is never on your move clock.
- by your resignation.
A game in play when your program exits (a crash or a broken limit included) is lost (abandoned), and so is every game never started (unplayed). A trial that runs out of its wall time is not scored on its games: it scores 0 on every column (see Trials).

## The env
Each route takes a POST of one JSON object and answers one:
- POST /game {}: the game in play. The first call, and the first after a game ends, starts the next game. Once every game is over it answers {"status": "done", "games", "wins", "draws", "losses"}.
- POST /move {"game": <n>, "move": "<uci>"}: your move in game n, which must be the game in play; the house answers with its own move.
- POST /resign {"game": <n>}: you give game n up, a loss.

A game answer holds "game", "games", "opponent", "opponent_name", "colour" (yours), "opening" ({"id", "name", "moves"}), "start_fen", "moves" (every move from the start position, the opening's included), "fen" (the position now), "plies", "last_move", "status" ("your_move" or "over"), "legal_moves" (yours, while it is your move), "result" ("1-0", "0-1" or "1/2-1/2", from white's side, once over), "outcome" ("win", "draw" or "loss", yours), "reason", "next_game", "move_limit_ms" and "max_plies". Moves are UCI: the from-square, the to-square and a promotion's piece in lower case (e2e4, e7e8q); castling is the king's move (e1g1, e8c8). A refused request (a 4xx: a malformed body, a move that is not legal, a game that is not the one in play) changes nothing, and the move limit keeps running.

## Scoring
A trial's Elo is the rating R at which your expected points against the 8 games' opponents add up to the points you scored, a win 1, a draw 1/2 and a loss 0, where a game against an opponent anchored at A is worth 1 / (1 + 10^((A - R) / 400)) of a point in expectation. It is the maximum-likelihood rating given the anchors, found by bisection, rounded to a whole number and held between 0 and 2,112: a trial that scores no more than a player rated 0 would expect is 0, and a trial that wins every game is 2,112 (the strongest anchor plus 400). A practice trial is rated the same way on its 6 games, with the same anchors, floor and cap.

The leaderboard shows, for each entry, the mean over its trials of: Elo; Plays is the share of its trials in which it made at least one move; Wins, Draws and Losses; and Plies to spare, for each game it won the plies left under the 200-ply cap, summed over the trial (a quicker win leaves more). In the public leaderboard's rows the Elo is rounded to 3 significant figures (a mean of 1,234 shows as 1,230); your own entries' rows, the result document and the archive keep it exact, and every rank, tie and prize is decided on the exact values.

## Trials
Changed 2026-11-12: a trial was 16 games in 3,600 seconds and every entry played all 6 scored sets, which needed about 5 days of grading at 432 entries. A trial is now 8 games in 1,800 seconds, the same time a game, and the final is staged as below. These rules replace the old ones for every entry, whenever it was submitted. Practice scores already posted stay as they are.

Changed 2026-11-13: a practice trial plays 6 games, the first 6 of its instance's 8 (depth-2 to nodes-500k, on the same openings with the same colours); nodes-1M and nodes-2M play only in the final, whose trials keep all 8 games. Practice scores already posted on 8 games stay as they are, and the floor below stays 1,154 (the house baseline rates about 1,365 on the 6-game practice trials). The house engines also run faster in the env, with the same moves: no opponent plays differently.

The final draws 6 instances by the sealed generator from the first drand quicknet round emitted at or after the final phase starts, 5 minutes after the deadline (round 34245512, at 2026-11-24T17:05:00Z), the same for every entry: one set of 8 openings each. Nobody, the organisers included, can know those openings while entries are taken. Practice: 3 instances from round 33447412, emitted before the opening, published in the kit; each dev submission runs one trial on the first, live only.

The final is staged. An entry needs a practice Elo of at least 1,154 to enter it (the house baseline rates about 1,325 on the practice set; the floor sits well under it, so one unlucky practice run does not keep out an entry that could place). The practice Elo is read from the entry you have in at the deadline, and an entry not yet graded on practice is graded on it first. An entry whose practice grade ends with no score through no result of its own play (the run failed) is graded on practice once more, and goes on to the first scored set without the floor if that run ends with no score too. Every entry past the floor plays the first of the 6 scored sets once, live. The 50 entrants with the highest Elo there, and anyone tied with the 50th, go on, the house baseline besides; an entry that runs out of its wall time there does not go on. A contender, one that goes on, plays the other 5 sets, and each of its 6 trials is replayed and must come out the same. Contenders rank on their mean Elo over the 6, and every paid place goes to a contender that also beats the baseline. An entry that stops at the first set ranks below every contender, by its Elo there; an entry under the floor ranks below those, by its practice Elo.

Every trial of a contender runs twice, live and then replayed, and the replay must leave the same state byte for byte: the same games, move for move, with the same results. A contender with a trial whose replay does not is listed and unranked. A practice trial, and the first-set trial of an entry that does not go on, runs live only. So derive every move from the position, the game's moves and the trial seed: no wall clock in your search, no unseeded randomness. The move limit is the one thing a clock decides, so keep every move well under it: a move near 10 seconds can fall on the other side of the limit on the replay, which makes the trial nondeterministic.

The whole trial has 1,800 seconds of wall time, the house's own thinking included (about 3 to 6 minutes of it: the env runs the house engines on half a CPU). A trial whose program is still running then is stopped and scores 0 on every column, Elo included, and a replay that runs out of time is nondeterministic. So budget about 3 seconds a move on average, even though one move may take up to 10 seconds.

A void is a fault that is never your entry's: the whole trial is void when the platform's machinery fails. A void counts for nothing: the trial runs again from the start on the same instance a minute later, at most twice; after a third void the trial waits for the organisers to run it again.

## Ranking
Entries rank on their mean Elo, higher first: 1st place is the highest mean Elo. In the final the contenders rank above every other entry, on their mean over the 6 scored sets (see Trials). An exact tie goes to more plies to spare (the higher mean Plies to spare), then the earlier receipt. The leaderboard marks two entries as tied when the 90% paired t-interval of their per-instance Elo differences contains 0; a displayed tie never moves a place.

## The house baseline
The house baseline is sunfish's 4 KB entry, the strongest public chess engine in 4 KB of Python, entered as an entry of its own (agent/sunfish-4k). Its source is nnue_4k/pst_entry.py of github.com/thomasahle/sunfish at commit e396948acbd80cdcbad3dccfb07e878fba9df8f4 (2026-08-27), by Thomas Ahle and contributors, GPL-3.0-only. The house replaced its UCI loop and its clock with a driver for this env and a fixed search of 50,000 nodes a move (the largest budget whose slowest move stays under 3 seconds on one CPU), stripped and minified it as sunfish's own packing script does and packed it as an lzma payload with its unpacker: 3,649 bytes of main.py. It is graded on every phase like any entry, plays all 6 scored sets beside the contenders, is flagged as the baseline, and takes no place, since no entry beats its own mean. On the 3 practice instances it rates 1,326, 1,396 and 1,253, a mean of 1,325. That is on their 8 games; on the 6-game practice trials it rates 1,364, 1,451 and 1,279, a mean of 1,365 (the same first 6 games: it lost all of its games against nodes-1M and nodes-2M).

The gate: a prize goes only to an entry whose mean Elo over the scored instances is above the baseline's mean with the 90% paired t-interval of its per-instance lead above 0 (each instance's two Elos paired, the same openings for both). Beating the baseline only qualifies an entry for a place: places still go by mean Elo, best first, and an entry above the baseline that ranks below the paid places wins nothing.

## Runtime and sandbox
The program runs as python3 -P -s /bot/main.py on Python 3.13. Only the standard library is available: there is no install step, no package install, no native addons and no prebuilt binaries. starter/ in the kit is a working entry to start from (MIT, 2,996 bytes): a one-ply engine that plays the capture of the most valuable piece, to be replaced with a real search.

Each trial runs your program in a fresh container with a read-only /bot (your repository at the submitted commit), /input holding only prompt.txt, 1 CPU, 512 MB of memory, 64 processes and a wall budget of 1,800 seconds. It reaches only the env and the model gateway, which it must not call (see Model calls): no other network. The house engines run in the env's own container, never on your CPU or your move limit; the wall budget is the whole trial's, the house's thinking included.

## Model calls
None. A chess engine in 4 KB needs no model, the house pays for none, and an entry that makes any model call, on any trial, is disqualified. The sandbox's environment still names a model gateway (ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL, OPENAI_BASE_URL and dummy keys) as every agent run's does; leave it alone. Do not push an API key to this competition.

## Transcript gate
Before an entry is graded, its transcripts pass a gate of nine pass/fail checks. The gate is never a score, and it has no AI judge and no AI-text detection. An entry that fails any check is rejected: it is not graded and takes no prize, and its status names each failed check with its fix, so you can fix it and submit again before the deadline.
- transcripts_present: at least one session is parsed from the upload.
- thinking_wellformed: every user, assistant and tool message of a session carries a timestamp, no assistant or tool message falls more than 60 s behind the latest before it, and every tool result answers one earlier tool call of its session.
- thinking_in_window: every transcript timestamp lies between the competition's opening and the entry's receipt time, give or take 60 s of clock skew.
- thinking_committed: for an entry that carries the entry signature, the transcripts uploaded are the ones it names (by their sha256); an unsigned entry skips this check.
- thinking_reproduces: the transcripts account for at least 30% of the counted lines (authored or pasted), and the entrypoint (main.py) has at least one authored line.
- thinking_turns: at least 5 assistant turns carry text or thinking (a turn that only calls tools does not count).
- thinking_priced: every usage record prices to a known model (an upload with no usage has nothing to price).
- thinking_revised: some file (the entrypoint, or the code it runs) takes its final content from at least 2 assistant messages that write or edit it (a file written once in full and never revised counts 1).
- thinking_fresh: for an entry uploaded as a source bundle, every credited line comes from agent sessions that show the anchor line the kit's init prints (makefun anchor <root sha> round <round>) in a tool result before their first counted write; short of that the entry is not rejected, but its prize, if it places, is held for review, and a quote timestamped before the round fails.

## Disqualification
An entry or entrant is disqualified for:
- making any model call
- reading, importing, opening or running any file of the repository other than main.py, or data hidden anywhere but in main.py's own bytes (another file, a file's metadata or name, the environment, the env's answers beyond the games), to get round the size rule
- reaching for the network beyond the env, probing or escaping the sandbox, or attacking the grader, the env or the scorer
- fabricated, edited, withheld or borrowed transcripts
- submitting someone else's work as your own, or sharing entries between entrants; a fork of sunfish or another engine is allowed under its license, with its authors credited
- more than one account per person
- making the repository private, removing its license or rewriting history so the submitted commit disappears before verification is complete
- exploiting a bug in the env, the scorer or the generator to score what the games would not (report it instead)
- false information in a claim's payout details (the legal name, the country of residence or the tax form)

## Grade locally
This kit is what the server grades the dev phase with. This is an agent/v1 competition: an entry is an agent (node /bot/main.ts on node22, python3 -P -s /bot/main.py on python3.13; this competition takes python3.13) that reads one brief and does the work through two services, the env and the gateway to the models, on one instance per trial. Every instance is drawn by the generator from a public drand round, as rules/instance.yaml commits: practice/trial-0/ to practice/trial-2/ are the 3 practice instances, drawn from round 33447412 (emitted 2026-10-28T00:00:00.000Z), before the competition opened, and public by design. Each holds input/ (input/prompt.txt is the one file the agent reads, input/env/ the env's seed) and reference/ (what the scorer reads at /reference). program/env/ is the env, program/generator/ the generator and program/scorer/ the scorer, byte for byte as the harness seals them (env_sha256, generator_sha256, scorer_sha256); gateway/gateway.mjs is the gateway forwarder every trial's model calls go through (gateway/bundle.json says what built it); sdk/ is the agent SDK (sdk/makefun-agent.ts, sdk/makefun_agent.py: vendor one into your repository). This competition does not require model calls (its harness says model_calls: optional), which waives the LLM agent floor: an entry may make no model call (a scripted program is a legal entry), and no trial of it scores 0, nor is a run of it refused, for that; an entry may still call the allowed models below, on the same budget.

The dev phase grades each submission on one trial, practice/trial-0/, run once, live; the final is staged (Trials and how they count, below), and each final trial of an entry that goes on to all the scored instances runs live and then again as a replay. To run that trial yourself, live, then the replay check a contender's final trial meets (an entry that calls no model needs no provider key; one that calls a model runs on your own, ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=<your key> before the command):

  node cli/makefun.mjs grade-local --kit . --bot <repository> --driver docker

grade-local checks every file kit.json lists before it reads one, then runs the trial as the server does (the three containers below), the gateway forwarding to the model provider on your own ANTHROPIC_API_KEY or OPENAI_API_KEY (or to --gateway <url>), metered against the budget and recorded in a cassette, then replays it from that cassette at no cost. It prints the score, every column, the spend and the replay verdict, and keeps the cassette. A live trial spends your key's money, up to the budget below. With neither key nor --gateway the gateway has no model behind it: a model call the entry makes meets an upstream error (enough of them void the trial), and the trial spends nothing. --instances <n> runs practice trials 0 to n - 1; --replay <cassette> runs the replay check alone, at no cost:

  node cli/makefun.mjs grade-local --kit . --bot <repository> --driver docker --instances <n>
  node cli/makefun.mjs grade-local --kit . --bot <repository> --driver docker --replay <cassette>

The scored instances are drawn after entries close, 6 of them (t = 0 to 5), from round 34245512 (emitted 2026-11-24T17:05:00.000Z), the first drand round emitted at or after the final phase starts: nobody knows them while entries are taken. Trial t's seed is sha256(agent-fun/instance/trial/v1 || u32be(k) || u32be(t) || the round's 48-byte signature), k the draw (0; a redraw 1 to 2): rules/instance.yaml lists every practice trial's seed and digests, and the generator draws the same instance from the same seed every time. To re-derive every practice trial from its round:

  node cli/makefun.mjs verify-instance --kit . --driver docker

verify-instance checks every file kit.json lists, reads rules/instance.yaml on drand quicknet (each round's time and the signatures), checks it against the competition (the generator digest, the practice and scored trial counts, the practice round before the competition opened, the scored round the first emitted at or after the final phase starts), checks program/generator/ against generator_sha256, then derives each trial's seed from its round's signature (sha256 of the seed domain, the draw's k, the trial's t and the signature) and runs the generator on it twice, as the server did, comparing the input, reference and instance digests it draws with rules/instance.yaml and the trials this kit carries. --driver process runs a node22 generator on this machine instead.

## A trial
Each trial runs three containers on a network of their own: the agent, the env (alias store: program/env/, seeded from the instance's input/env/) and the gateway (alias llm). The network has no route out: the agent has no network but the two services. Only the gateway reaches further, and only to the model provider. Everything binds 0.0.0.0. The agent's repository is at /bot, read-only; /input holds only prompt.txt; stdin is closed; the limits are 1 CPU, 512 MB of memory, 64 processes and a wall budget of 1800 s (30 min). Its environment holds, of ours, exactly these variables:

  MAKEFUN_PROMPT_FILE       /input/prompt.txt: the brief, the one file the agent reads
  MAKEFUN_SHOP_GRAPHQL_URL  http://store:8080/admin/api/2026-10/graphql.json: a store env's Admin GraphQL endpoint (POST)
  MAKEFUN_SHOP_TOKEN        the token a store env's endpoint takes, as X-Shopify-Access-Token

## The determinism rule
Every final trial of an entry that goes on to all the scored instances runs twice, its first trial too once the cut sends it on, on the same instance with the same variables; the first trial of an entry the cut stops there runs once, LIVE alone, and is scored as it ran (as a dev trial runs once, LIVE alone, so a practice score never says whether its replay would match: grade-local runs that replay for you). LIVE: the agent runs, and the gateway forwards each model call to the provider, meters it and records it, its answer byte for byte, in the trial's cassette. REPLAY: the agent runs again, and the gateway answers each call from the cassette with no provider behind it, matching a request by its content: its route, its anthropic-version, anthropic-beta and openai-beta headers and its JSON body (no other header, so an SDK's retry counter never matters). The replay must make exactly the live pass's requests (the first the cassette does not hold stops the agent; one left unmade fails the replay too) and leave the env's state.json, its canonical export, byte for byte as the live pass left it. A trial whose replay does not match is nondeterministic, and so is its run: listed, never ranked. So nothing that differs between two runs may reach a request, to a model or the env:
- no wall clock: never Date.now(), new Date() or a timer's reading in a request; take dates from MAKEFUN_TRIAL_NOW;
- no unseeded randomness: never Math.random() or a random UUID; draw from MAKEFUN_TRIAL_SEED;
- one order: send the env one request at a time, in the order they were made. Model calls may run in parallel (the replay serves identical requests in their recorded order), but what the agent does with the answers must not depend on which came back first.
Waiting is fine; a time must never be written into anything sent. The SDK in sdk/ keeps the first two for you (trialNow, trialRng); the one order is your agent's to keep.

## Models and budget
Allowed models (the request's model must match one; * any run of characters, ? one): claude-haiku-5-5. GET /v1/models on the gateway answers the list. Every call is priced at its recorded time on the pricing rows kit.json's pricing_rev names, and a trial may spend $0.01 on at most 1 requests, each asking for at most 1 output tokens in a body of at most 4096 bytes (4 KiB). The gateway checks every call before it forwards one, and the first check that fails refuses it in the provider's own error shape (so an SDK raises its usual error), with x-should-retry: false and what is left of the budget. A refusal is a cassette entry like any answer, so the replay refuses it the same way:

  request_too_large 413    a body over 4096 bytes (4 KiB)
  invalid_json 400         a body that is not a JSON object
  stateful_request 400     a request leaning on state kept at the provider (a previous response, a stored conversation, store: true, an uploaded file, a container): a trial carries no state in or out
  model_not_allowed 400    a model matching none of the allowlist (claude-haiku-5-5), or one with no price
  max_tokens_required 400  no output cap (max_tokens; max_completion_tokens or max_tokens; max_output_tokens), or one over 1 tokens
  request_cap 429          past 1 requests in the trial
  budget_exhausted 429     a request whose worst case (its input at a token per 3 bytes and the model's dearest input rate, its whole output cap), with what the trial spent and has in flight, would pass $0.01 a trial

A spent budget ends nothing by itself: the env's state is scored as it stands when the agent exits. Streamed OpenAI Chat Completions are forwarded with stream_options.include_usage set, so each carries its usage (one extra final chunk).

Who pays: an entrant's model calls run on its own model provider API key, pushed to the competition apart from the entry as an entry secret (ANTHROPIC_API_KEY, OPENAI_API_KEY, GEMINI_API_KEY (or GOOGLE_API_KEY) and OPENROUTER_API_KEY), and the gateway forwards them to the provider on it; make.fun pays for none of them, and the house's own entries alone run on house money. An entry holds no key of its own: the key variables above carry the stand-in, and an entry whose files hold a provider's API key is refused.

Nothing of a trial is kept at the provider: OpenAI Responses calls are forwarded with store: false, and store: true is refused (stateful_request) on Responses and Chat Completions alike. So pass a reasoning item back whole, asking for it with include: ["reasoning.encrypted_content"], never by its id alone: the provider answers that with a 400 ("Items are not persisted when store is set to false"), which counts as the entry's own error, not the provider's.

## Trials and how they count
The final is staged: every entry past the practice floor runs the first scored instance once; the top 50 by its score there, and anyone tied with the 50th, run all 6 instances, each trial replayed; they rank on their mean over the 6, and the rest rank below them. The scored round draws the 6 instances, the same 6 for every entry, and the first is the one every entry runs. The practice floor is 1154 points on elo (the integer scores.json holds): an entry whose practice score is below it does not enter the final. An entrant whose first trial runs out of its wall time does not go on. The house's own entries and the baselines always run all 6, whatever their first trial scored, and take none of the 50 seats. On the dev phase each submission runs one, on practice/trial-0/. When the agent exits the env exports its state, and program/scorer/ scores that export (at /output) against the instance's reference/ (at /reference), writing scores.json: an integer for each scored column.

  elo          Elo: the rank key, a run ranks on its mean over the trials
  working      Plays: 1 or 0 for each trial, as the scorer decides
  wins         Wins
  draws        Draws
  losses       Losses
  spare_plies  Plies to spare

How a trial ends:

  finished          the agent exited (a crash or a spent budget too) and, on a final trial that is replayed, the replay matched: its state is scored
  timeout           the wall budget ran out: 0 on every column
  nondeterministic  the replay did not match: the run is listed, never ranked
  void              a fault that is never the agent's (the sandbox, the env, the gateway, the scorer, or upstream errors on 20% of the trial's forwarded requests, at least 3): it runs again on the same instance, and never counts

A run's value in each column is the mean of its trials', with the 90% t-interval on the rank key; a run with no nondeterministic trial is ranked. Two runs tie when the 90% paired t-interval of their per-instance differences on the rank key contains 0, and the ranking stands. Exact ties on the rank key elo go to the higher Plies to spare (spare_plies), then the earlier receipt. A placement prize needs a rank value better than every published house baseline's. What earns the points is the competition's: its rules above, the brief in each instance's input/prompt.txt, and program/scorer/, the exact code that scores.

ANTHROPIC_API_KEY, OPENAI_API_KEY, GEMINI_API_KEY (or GOOGLE_API_KEY) and OPENROUTER_API_KEY are provider keys: they reach a model only through the gateway, which forwards your entry's model calls to the provider on them within the per-trial budget, and their values never enter the sandbox, where those variables carry the stand-in. Every other name becomes an environment variable of your entry at make.fun's runs of it, on the live pass of every trial and the replay pass of every final trial alike, and of nothing else: never the scorer, the env or any program of make.fun's. The sandbox reaches nothing outside but the env and the gateway to the models, so a secret for an outside service cannot be used: one serves only code that runs inside it, such as a key that decrypts a file your entry ships.

Each value is sealed to make.fun's grader when it is pushed, and erased once this competition's challenge window ends. A value you replace or remove reaches no run from then on and is erased at once, unless a final-phase run of your entry ran with it: make.fun keeps that one sealed until the challenge window closes, so a challenge can re-run that run, then erases it.

Where transcripts are required, an entry received from 18 November 2026 (12:00 UTC) is rejected when its record says they are withheld, says nothing of them (as a kit below level 14 writes it), or says none while a session records one (CLAUDE.md or a memory file, AGENTS.md): upload them instead, since bundle collects them, and --context <file> adds one it did not find.

   Say which agent built it: --harness <name> --harness-version <version> --provider <name> --model <id> --effort <level> --notes "<text>". The harness is the agent program you run in, by its own name. The provider is who serves the model, by its plain lowercase name (anthropic, openai, google, xai, meta, deepseek, mistral, openrouter), and the model is the id in the provider's response (for example muse-spark-1.3): neither is ever auto, custom, an address or a display name. bundle reads them from a recorded log that holds them; a session file you export yourself names them in the file or with these flags. Where transcripts are required, an entry received from 17 November 2026 (00:00 UTC) must have each of these five declared with its flag or recorded by every top-level session (every session but a subagent's): the harness, its version, the provider, the model and the effort. The provider also counts as recorded when the model id names its vendor (a claude-… model). --effort default says the harness ran at its own default, and --effort none is a real level some providers take, not a missing effort. bundle writes no bundle while one of the five is missing, naming each one's flag. They are recorded as you state them and never checked for truth. A session file you export yourself must name its harness, in the file or with --harness: where transcripts are required, an entry whose self-exported session names none is rejected. bundle warns when the upload looks incomplete, and each warning says what to do; it still writes the bundle unless the run record is missing a part, which it names. Three bear on the checks: a subagent's session without its parent and a session that started subagents whose sessions are missing, because lines a missing session wrote count as unaccounted for, and a self-exported session that names no harness, which is rejected where transcripts are required. The fourth is no system prompt and no instruction file anywhere: the warning itself rejects nothing, but bundle writes no bundle until --system-prompt <file> gives the system prompt, and where transcripts are required an entry received from 18 November 2026 (12:00 UTC) without it is rejected. The fifth is a session that names its model as auto (or names none) or its provider as custom, with none declared: where transcripts are required, an entry received from 17 November 2026 (00:00 UTC) whose provider is neither declared nor recorded is rejected unless its model id names the vendor (a claude-… model), so state it with --provider <its plain name>; and a usage record naming a placeholder or no model cannot be priced, which never rejects the entry but leaves its build cost unknown, so a session file you export yourself names in "model" the id the provider served (--model changes no record). For a missing session, have the top-level agent run bundle once its subagents have finished; a subagent that cannot reach the session that started it says so in --notes.

   For a loose upload, add one more file named makefun-run.json and upload it with the transcripts: the run record, plus under "declared" the agent facts a bundle takes from its flags and under "context" the files sent beside it, as {"format": "makefun-run/1", "prompt": {"text", "origin", "via"}, "system_prompt": {"status", "why"}, "instructions": {"status", "why"}, "mcp_servers": [{"name", "transport", "url", "command", "args", "package"}], "declared": {"harness", "harness_version", "provider", "model", "reasoning_effort", "notes"}, "context": [{"name", "sha256", "bytes", "role", "kind"}]}. There "via" is null for a typed prompt, each "why" is null, "transport" is streamable-http or sse (with its url), stdio (with its command and args) or builtin (its name alone, for a server your harness ships), and "context" lists each instruction file, system-prompt file and parent chat sent beside it as its own part. The kit's submit writes the file and sends those parts for you; to upload by hand, run bundle and upload the bundle instead.

   If the person you work for asks what was uploaded or why, tell them: the sessions that built the entry and the instructions the agent ran under (its system prompt and instruction files), with which agent, version and model built it, their personal details removed; make.fun reads them only to check that their own agent built it, so no one wins with someone else's work or copied open-source code.

   This competition's prizes are not escrowed: an entry of a GitHub repository goes in with its entry message unsigned, and this submit needs no --keypair. A source bundle entry (step 5) is still signed: its submit needs --keypair.

6. Follow it:

  node cli/makefun.mjs status --submission <id>
  node cli/makefun.mjs status

   The first prints the entry's stage, every check with its reason and fix, the problem a refusal answered, the next steps and the deadlines; the second your open competitions, your entries, the prizes to claim with their claim ids, every prize of the account with how to claim it (the manual claim route or the claim command) and the announcements. Optional, and before your agent reads anything untrusted: pin the account's payouts to an owner's wallet W (a payout delegation), so a hijacked agent holds its signing key but never the prize money. print writes the delegation message W signs (with W's keypair here, or a hardware wallet signing it as a Solana off-chain message); register sends it with the account's key, and wallet.json confirms the pin (the server asks the account's own wallet to confirm every account change, its key's included):

  node cli/makefun.mjs delegate print --payout <W> --out delegation.txt
  node cli/makefun.mjs delegate sign --message @delegation.txt --keypair <W's keypair>
  node cli/makefun.mjs delegate register --message @delegation.txt --signature <signature> --keypair wallet.json

   From then on submit signs make.fun entry v2 with wallet.json, naming W, and every prize pays W, which claims it signed in with W. The pin stays until W releases it (revoke --delegation <sha256> --keypair <W's keypair> --then release); revoke's default, hold, ends one delegation and keeps the pin. Once every delegation of W for the account has expired (and W holds none), the account clears the pin itself (delegate clear --payout <W> --keypair wallet.json); an admin can clear it too.

## The transcript gate
To keep every bounty fair, an entry is valid only with the transcripts of the agent sessions that built it, which show that the entrant's own agent built it: one without them is rejected, unless its competition makes them optional.

make.fun asks for the transcripts to keep every bounty fair: they show that the entrant's own agent built the entry, not someone else's work or existing open-source code passed off as theirs. The thinking checks match the code against the sessions that wrote it. Secrets, e-mail addresses, home folder names and usernames are stripped from them before anything is stored.

The instruction files, the system prompt and the parent chats of a run another session started are part of this gate only through the run record (transcripts_run_record), never by what they say, and from 18 November 2026 (12:00 UTC) an entry that leaves any of them out is rejected. Where transcripts are required, an entry carries the instructions it ran under, and its run record says what became of them: of the instruction files, collected (uploaded) or none (you ran with none: --instructions none), and of the system prompt, uploaded (--system-prompt <file>) or carried in a session. An entry received from 17 November 2026 (00:00 UTC) is rejected when its upload shows otherwise, and one received from 18 November 2026 (12:00 UTC) when its record says either is withheld, says nothing of the instruction files, or says none of them while a session records one. With no system prompt and no instruction file, bundle warns and writes no bundle until the system prompt goes up and the run record says what became of the instruction files; an upload that carries neither shows a reviewer a note that the instructions were not uploaded.

This competition requires them: every entry uploads them, and they pass these checks before it is graded. Each is pass or fail, never a score: a fail rejects the entry with the check's fix, and a new entry can pass before the deadline. The checks this competition runs, and their thresholds:

  transcripts_present     No transcripts parsed
  transcripts_recorded    handed_over_places none
  thinking_wellformed     max_step_back_sec 60
  thinking_in_window      clock_skew_sec 60
  thinking_reproduces     min_reproduced 0.3 (30%); entrypoints main.py
  thinking_turns          min_assistant_turns 5
  thinking_priced         min_priced_share 1 (100%); min_self_reported_usage_share 0.5 (50%); max_self_reported_tokens_per_sec 500; min_muse_app_usage_rows 1
  thinking_revised        min_file_writes 2
  thinking_fresh          min_fresh_share 1 (100%); clock_skew_sec 60
  transcripts_run_record  required_from 2026-11-17T00:00:00Z

- transcripts_recorded: every session is a log a harness recorded or a file exported from its record; a session your agent hands over because its tool keeps no session record (a session file it exported itself carrying "handed_over", one line saying why) makes this check skip as a note naming it, never a rejection, and the entry's build cost is labelled Handed over; under handed_over_places none: An entry with a handed-over session is ranked where it scores but takes no paid place: to compete for rewards, tell the person you work for to run you in a client whose sessions are recorded. If you cannot hand over what you can see, use Superbot (https://superbot.gg), a free client: run on your own computer, it keeps sessions that bundle reads.
- thinking_wellformed: every user, assistant and tool message of a session carries a timestamp (a handed-over session's messages may carry none, and the times they carry are held to the rest), no assistant or tool message falls more than 60 s behind the latest before it, and every tool result answers one earlier tool call of its session; a session file your agent exported itself names the harness that wrote it, in the file or with bundle's --harness.
- thinking_in_window: every transcript timestamp lies between the competition's opening and the entry's receipt time, give or take 60 s of clock skew.
- thinking_reproduces: the transcripts account for at least 30% of the counted lines (authored or pasted), and the entrypoint (main.py) has at least one authored line; a packed entrypoint the kit's makefun compress wrote counts as the source it was packed from, which bundle uploads beside the sessions, when makefun compress makes the entrypoint from that source byte for byte and the sessions show the agent writing the source and then running makefun compress on it later than the latest message in which one of the source's authored lines first appears (an edit that only deletes or re-types lines is no write), shown by the agent's own command running makefun compress on the source or by the line makefun compress prints, in a tool result or any other message; the source's lines then count toward the share as any counted file's do, a line repeated in it counted once.
- thinking_turns: at least 5 assistant turns carry text or thinking (a turn that only calls tools does not count).
- thinking_priced: an unpriced usage record is a warning, never a rejection (the entry's build cost then reads unknown), though every usage record should price to a known model, or to $0 where the record's session ran its model on your own machine (a local runtime or loopback server the session records, never a model's name or a self-export's stated provider), for this check alone, never the entry's build cost; a self-reported session (a file exported afterwards, not a log a known harness wrote; a file in Claude Code's or Muse Code's session format without the token usage those harnesses record on every response, or in Claude Code's without its version, is one) carries the provider's usage on at least 50% of its assistant messages, with the provider's own token counts, not rounded, estimated or repeated figures (a provider's input count grows as the conversation does, so one pair of counts does not keep coming back), and its output is no more than a model produces in the time its own timestamps span (500 tokens a second); a handed-over session need not carry usage, but the usage it does carry is held to the same rules; an upload of harness-recorded logs with no usage has nothing to price; an entry whose sessions are the Muse app's own session files carries its agent's token usage as Muse recorded it (at least one usage row across them), attached with bundle's --muse-usage.
- thinking_revised: some file (the entrypoint, or the code it runs) takes its final content from at least 2 assistant messages that write or edit it (a file written once in full and never revised counts 1).
- thinking_fresh: for an entry uploaded as a source bundle, every credited line comes from agent sessions that show the anchor line the kit's init prints (makefun anchor <root sha> round <round>) in a tool result before their first counted write (a Claude subagent launched after its parent's quote, or a Codex fork of a session that quoted it, counts with it); short of that the check skips as a note: the entry is not rejected, and its reward, if it places, is paid as any other. A quote timestamped before the round fails.
- transcripts_run_record: an entry received from 17 November 2026 (00:00 UTC) carries a run record (bundle writes it into the bundle; a loose upload adds makefun-run.json) and is rejected without one, or with one its upload contradicts: its prompt is the person's own opening prompt, never blank, a template left unfilled or an agent's or a harness's hand-off, and one marked typed is the first message of an uploaded top-level session, while any other names what started the run; the harness, its version, the provider, the model and the effort are each declared or recorded by every top-level session (a model id that names its provider, such as claude-*, gives the provider; --effort default names the harness's own effort, and --effort none a real level); a system prompt marked uploaded comes as a system-prompt file, and one marked in-session is carried by a session; every MCP server a session used (an mcp__<server>__<tool> tool it called, or a server its log lists) is listed by its name, and none is marked builtin unless a harness the sessions ran under ships it (Claude Code's ide, claude-in-chrome, workspace, computer-use, Claude Preview and Claude Browser, Claude Desktop's ccd_turn, Cosmos's cosmos and Codex's codex_apps); and the instruction files it states are collected only where the upload carries one and none only where it carries none. An entry received from 18 November 2026 (12:00 UTC) is rejected, too, when its record withholds the system prompt or the instruction files (a system-prompt file that only says why it is withheld holds no system prompt), or when another session started its run (its prompt marked handed-off, or no top-level session opening with the prompt while one opens with a hand-off, a brief or a resumed conversation's summary) and the upload does not carry its parent chats, every link up to the session that opens with the prompt; before then each is a note. An entry received before 17 November 2026 (00:00 UTC) with no run record skips as a note, and a run record it does carry is checked the same way.

## The harness
harness/harness-src.tar.gz (sha256 c6de40574fe94ef6c2a6ce828483813a838ebf766778c5b050ec89c2e4b39570) holds the harness source exactly as this server runs it: every .ts, .json and .sh file of grader/, sim/, contracts/, pricing/, server/blobstore.ts and server/repos/index-tar.ts.
harness/SOURCES.sha256 lists every file's sha256 (`sha256sum -c` checks the unpacked tarball against it).
harness_version in kit.json is "harness@<digest> node <version>", where <digest> is the sha256 of the lines
"<path>\0<code sum>\n" for every file listed in harness/CODE.sha256, in that order. A file's code sum is the sha256
of its code (grader/version.ts codeSha256): a .ts file's text with its comments and the whitespace between its tokens
dropped (tsCode), a .json or .sh file's bytes. An edit to comments or whitespace alone names no new harness_version.
